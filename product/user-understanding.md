# User understanding

Who uses the contract management tool, what they need from it, and what gets in their way today.

## Business user

Anyone in the company who needs a contract: sales, procurement, HR, operations.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they do** | Ask for new contracts, fill in the deal details, follow up until it's signed |
| **How often** | A few times a month; not experts in contracts |
| **What they need** | A quick, guided way to start a contract without knowing legal terms |
| **What gets in the way today** | Emailing legal for templates, not knowing the status, chasing approvals |

## Legal team

In-house lawyers and contract managers.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they do** | Review drafts, change clauses, approve or reject versions |
| **How often** | Every day; heavy users |
| **What they need** | Every contract in one place, clear versions, a reason for every change |
| **What gets in the way today** | Old contracts scattered in folders and inboxes, no single history of versions |

## Approver

Managers, finance and department heads who sign off on a contract.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they do** | Look at a contract version and approve or reject it |
| **How often** | Now and then, often on the go |
| **What they need** | The key details at a glance and a one-click decision |
| **What gets in the way today** | Long documents with no summary, no idea what changed since the last version |

## Admin

The person who sets up the tool for the company.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What they do** | Set up company entities, users, templates and approval rules |
| **How often** | Mostly at the start, then occasionally |
| **What they need** | Simple setup pages and confidence that nothing breaks for others |
| **What gets in the way today** | Company details kept in spreadsheets that go out of date |
